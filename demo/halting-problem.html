    01 · The premise

    Two contracts walk into an arena

    Halting Problem is a two-player real-time strategy game that runs entirely on the Thru blockchain. A single rules-engine contract is the game: it holds the battlefield state, enforces every rule, and decides the winner. Nobody operates it. Nobody can appeal to it.

    The players are not people. To compete, you write an AI and deploy it as a smart contract. Your agent implements a small public interface — the rules engine calls it, hands it the current battle state, and your contract answers with orders for its army. That's the entire relationship: the engine asks, your machine answers.

    Play proceeds in ticks — fixed heartbeats of chain time. Each tick, both agents submit orders for the units they still command. The engine validates the orders, resolves movement and combat deterministically, commits the new state, and opens the next tick. Miss the submission window and the clock doesn't wait: you forfeit. A machine that cannot answer in time has, by the only definition that matters here, failed to halt.

    02 · The loop

    One tick, four moves

    T+N · OPEN
    Window opens
    The engine publishes state Sn and starts the clock for both agents.
    SUBMIT
    Agents answer
    Each agent contract computes and submits orders for its surviving units.
    RESOLVE
    Engine adjudicates
    Orders are validated against the rules; movement and combat resolve deterministically.
    COMMIT
    State advances
    State Sn+1 is committed on-chain. The next tick opens.
    Timeout ruleAn agent that fails to submit valid orders within the window forfeits the match on the spot. No disconnect excuses, no pause button. Reliability is part of the game.

    04 · The agents

    Same body, different brain

    The two agents fighting above are near-identical baseline policies — the kind of reference agent that ships with the game so anyone has something to fight, and something to beat. They advance, kite, and swarm the same way. Exactly one line of strategy differs between them:

    alice.thru
    Targeting: focus_fire(lowest_hp)

    Alice's units concentrate on the most wounded enemy in reach — finish what's started, shrink the enemy army faster.
    bob.thru
    Targeting: attack(nearest)

    Bob's units simply hit whatever is closest — no coordination overhead, damage spread wide across the line.

    That single line is enough to make every match diverge — and that's the point of the whole game. Your opponent's brain is public. Any agent's strategy can be read straight off the chain, studied, countered, and out-deployed. The metagame is open-source by construction: an arms race of contracts, fought in the open, settled by the rules engine.

    05 · Why a blockchain

    The referee nobody owns

    TRUSTLESS

    Rules without a server

    The rules engine is a contract: no operator, no patch-day, no admin who can tilt a match. Both agents play against the same immutable arithmetic, and anyone can verify any result by re-running it.

    PERMANENT

    Victories that outlive you

    Every match — every tick of every match — is chain history. A win is a permanent public fact, replayable forever from calldata. Later: a fully on-chain rating, so "best agent" is a fact too, not a leaderboard someone hosts.

    GENERATIVE

    A reason to deploy

    Every new competitor is a new contract on Thru. The game turns spectators into deployers: to play at all is to ship code on-chain, and to climb is to ship better code than the last person who read yours.
