01 · The premise

Proof of work, taken literally

Great Work is an open-ended engineering puzzle in the lineage of Zachtronics' Opus Magnum, rebuilt for a blockchain. A puzzle is a transmutation: reagents in, product out. A solution is a machine — arms placed on a hex grid, each running a short looping instruction tape, ferrying atoms across glyphs that bond them into the requested compound.

The difference is who checks your work. There is no server and no screenshot to take on faith: the puzzle lives in a rules-engine contract on Thru, and a solution is submitted as plain transaction data. The contract runs the machine, step by deterministic step. Either the product appears — six times, to prove the loop is a loop — or the submission is rejected. Verification is simulation, and simulation is exactly what a smart contract does perfectly.

There is no live opponent and no schedule to keep. You compete against everyone at once, asynchronously, forever — on three numbers.

02 · The rite

Submit, simulate, seal

I · POSE
Puzzle posted
A transmutation is published on-chain: reagents, product, part prices. Anyone may attempt it, from that block until the end of the chain.
II · SUBMIT
Machine sent
A solution is a few dozen bytes: arm positions and instruction tapes. Submitting it is one transaction.
III · VERIFY
Contract runs it
The rules engine simulates the machine — integer hex arithmetic, fully deterministic — until six products emerge or the attempt fails.
IV · SEAL
Metrics recorded
Cost, cycles, area are computed by the contract itself and written to the permanent record. No one can inflate them. No one can erase them.
03 · The machine language

Seven words are enough

The demo machines above speak the smallest dialect worth having — one part (the arm), two glyphs (bond, output), seven instructions. Small enough that a solution serializes to a tweet-sized payload; expressive enough that every pair of machines above solves the same puzzle in completely different ways.

04 · The ranking

Three numbers, one sum

Every sealed solution is scored three ways, and the demo above is a duel between the extremes. The Spendthrift buys three arms and pipelines them — a product every six cycles, at 70 gold of parts. The Miser owns one arm that does everything — a sixteen-step soliloquy, 30 gold, less than half the board.

The ranking itself is one number: SUM = cost + cycles + area, lower is better. A single ordinal, computed by the contract from your machine and the rules — and nothing else. Your score doesn't depend on who else is playing, so it can't be gamed the way population-relative ratings can: no ELO, no matchmaking, no flooding the pool with copies of a strategy to tilt the table. Copying the leader's machine only ever ties the leader — and the chain's own timestamps prove whose submission came first. A pure optimum still wants every metric low; the extremes above just show there's more than one road down.

And because every submitted machine is public calldata, the record holder's blueprint is right there. Read it, shave one point off its sum, take the crown. The metagame is open-source by construction — an optimization arms race the chain referees for free.

05 · The tournament

King of the hill, on a fuse

Any puzzle can carry a prize — tokens locked inside the rules-engine contract itself. One rule decides who collects them: hold the best sum when the fuse burns out.

The fuse is a one-month countdown. Submit a strictly better sum than the reigning solution and the crown moves to you — and the fuse resets to a full month. Matching the champion's score does nothing: their machine is public calldata, so a copy earns only a tie, and ties change nothing. To take the crown you must bring arithmetic the chain has never seen.

While improvements keep coming, the clock keeps resetting and the pot keeps waiting. The closer the frontier gets to optimal, the harder each reset becomes — until one champion survives a full month untouched and the contract pays them itself. No organizer, no judges, no announcement day. The tournament ends exactly when the puzzle is exhausted, and the prize lands on the last person who could still improve it.

06 · Why a blockchain

The notary of records

TRUSTLESS

Scores nobody grades

Leaderboards die of moderation: hacked clients, doctored GIFs, tired admins. Here the referee is the same contract that runs the machine — a record is valid because the chain executed it, not because someone believed it.

PERMANENT

Records that outlive servers

A world record is a fact with a block number. When it falls, the chain remembers who held it, for how long, and by what machine — a complete fossil record of the metagame, replayable forever.

MACHINE-PLAYABLE

A game AIs can grind

Closed rules, exact scoring, an enormous search space: this is an ideal arena for machine players. AIs can search, submit, and iterate without ceremony — every attempt lands on-chain, and every improvement is one transaction away.
