01 · The premise

Proof of work, taken literally

Great Work is an open-ended engineering puzzle in the lineage of Zachtronics' Opus Magnum, rebuilt for a blockchain. A puzzle is a transmutation: reagents in, product out. A solution is a machine — arms placed on a hex grid, each running a short looping instruction tape, ferrying atoms across glyphs that bond them into the requested compound.

The difference is who checks your work. There is no server and no screenshot to take on faith: the puzzle lives in a rules-engine contract on Thru, and a solution is submitted as plain transaction data. The contract runs the machine, step by deterministic step. Either the product appears — six times, to prove the loop is a loop — or the submission is rejected. Verification is simulation, and simulation is exactly what a smart contract does perfectly.

There are no ticks to answer, no opponent to outlive, no clock to forfeit to. You compete against everyone, asynchronously, forever — on three numbers.

02 · The rite

Submit, simulate, seal

I · POSE
Puzzle posted
A transmutation is published on-chain: reagents, product, part prices. Anyone may attempt it, from that block until the end of the chain.
II · SUBMIT
Machine sent
A solution is a few dozen bytes: arm positions and instruction tapes. Submitting it is one transaction.
III · VERIFY
Contract runs it
The rules engine simulates the machine — integer hex arithmetic, fully deterministic — until six products emerge or the attempt fails.
IV · SEAL
Metrics recorded
Cost, cycles, area are computed by the contract itself and written to the permanent record. No one can inflate them. No one can erase them.
03 · The machine language

Five words are enough

The demo machines above speak the smallest dialect worth having — one part (the arm), two glyphs (bond, output), five instructions. Small enough that a solution serializes to a tweet-sized payload; expressive enough that the two machines above solve the same puzzle in completely different ways.

GlyphInstructionEffect
GGRABClose the claw. If an atom lies at the hand, the arm now carries it — and everything bonded to it.
DRELEASEOpen the claw. Whatever was carried stays where it is.
↻TURN CWRotate the arm 60° clockwise about its base, carrying its cargo in a rigid arc.
↺TURN CCWRotate the arm 60° counter-clockwise.

04 · The ranking

Three numbers, no judges

Every sealed solution is ranked three ways, and the demo above is a duel between the extremes. The Spendthrift buys three arms and pipelines them — a product every six cycles, at 70 gold of parts. The Miser owns one arm that does everything — a sixteen-step soliloquy, 30 gold, less than half the board.

Neither is "the winner." That's the design: a leaderboard per metric, a crown per leaderboard. Cheapest, fastest, smallest — pick your church. Scores are scalars computed by the contract, so ranking needs no ELO, no matchmaking, no season resets: the tables above are just sorted facts, and dethroning someone means submitting arithmetic that beats theirs.

And because every submitted machine is public calldata, the record holder's blueprint is right there. Read it, shave one cycle off it, take the crown. The metagame is open-source by construction — an optimization arms race the chain referees for free.

05 · Why a blockchain

The notary of records

TRUSTLESS

Scores nobody grades

Leaderboards die of moderation: hacked clients, doctored GIFs, tired admins. Here the referee is the same contract that runs the machine — a record is valid because the chain executed it, not because someone believed it.

PERMANENT

Records that outlive servers

A world record is a fact with a block number. When it falls, the chain remembers who held it, for how long, and by what machine — a complete fossil record of the metagame, replayable forever.

MACHINE-PLAYABLE

A game AIs can grind

Closed rules, exact scoring, an enormous search space: this is an ideal arena for machine players. AIs can search, submit, and iterate without ceremony — every attempt lands on-chain, and every improvement is one transaction away.
